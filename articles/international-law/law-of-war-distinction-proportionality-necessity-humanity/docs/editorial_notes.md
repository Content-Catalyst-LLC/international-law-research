# Editorial Notes

This article is designed as a doctrinal and practice-oriented deep dive into the law-of-war principles that govern conduct during armed conflict. It should be read after the broader Geneva Conventions article and before the occupation/detention article.

Editorial emphasis:

- Distinguish jus ad bellum questions from jus in bello rules.
- Treat civilian protection as the analytic center of the conduct-of-hostilities framework.
- Avoid treating military necessity as a free-floating override.
- Include contemporary complications such as urban warfare, hospitals, siege, cyber effects, drones, and AI targeting without turning the article into a technology essay.
