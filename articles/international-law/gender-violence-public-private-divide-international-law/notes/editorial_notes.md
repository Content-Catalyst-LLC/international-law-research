# Editorial Notes

This article should be framed as a critical but practical analysis of how international law recognizes gender-based violence across the public/private divide.

Avoid two oversimplifications:

1. Gender-based violence is not only a private harm committed by private actors.
2. International legal recognition is not enough unless institutions can prevent harm, protect survivors, investigate effectively, provide remedies, and address structural inequality.

The article should emphasize lawyer-facing analysis: identify the harm, actor, state connection, legal regime, protected right, evidentiary issue, procedural risk, remedy, and structural context. It should also preserve the critical point that the public/private divide has historically made domestic, sexual, familial, reproductive, economic, and intimate harms less visible as international legal problems.
