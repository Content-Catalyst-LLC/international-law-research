## Collective Security Concepts

- **Collective security** (system concept) — Institutionalized response to threats to peace through common legal mechanisms.
- **Security Council primary responsibility** (Charter principle) — Article 24 conferral of primary responsibility for peace and security on the Council.
- **Veto** (Security Council voting) — Power of permanent members to block substantive Security Council resolutions.
- **Chapter VI** (peaceful settlement) — Charter framework for peaceful settlement of disputes.
- **Chapter VII** (enforcement powers) — Charter framework for threats to peace breaches of peace and acts of aggression.
- **Article 39** (enforcement gateway) — Security Council determination of threat to peace breach of peace or aggression.
- **Article 41** (non-force measures) — Security Council measures not involving armed force.
- **Article 42** (force measures) — Security Council military enforcement measures when Article 41 is inadequate.
- **Article 51** (self-defense) — Inherent right of individual or collective self-defense if armed attack occurs.
- **Peacekeeping** (UN practice) — Operational tool developed beyond explicit Charter text through mandates and practice.
- **Peace enforcement** (UN practice) — Use or authorization of coercive force to restore peace and security.
- **Uniting for Peace** (General Assembly workaround) — General Assembly emergency procedure when Security Council is blocked.
- **Security Council paralysis** (structural problem) — Failure to act where permanent-member interests or alliances block decision.
- **Selective enforcement** (critical method) — Unequal application of collective-security mechanisms.
- **Palestine** (contemporary crisis) — Central case exposing gap between Charter principles and Security Council paralysis.
- **South Africa v Israel** (international adjudication) — Example of Global South legal action where collective-security machinery is blocked.
