# Doctrine Summary

The prohibition on the threat or use of force is the central rule governing resort to force in modern international law. Article 2(4) of the UN Charter prohibits force against the territorial integrity or political independence of any state or in any other manner inconsistent with the purposes of the United Nations.

The main recognized pathways for lawful force are Security Council authorization and self-defense under Article 51. Other asserted bases — humanitarian intervention, protection of nationals, counterterrorism across borders, proxy action, and consent-based deployments — require close doctrinal analysis because they can become vehicles for evading the Charter framework.
