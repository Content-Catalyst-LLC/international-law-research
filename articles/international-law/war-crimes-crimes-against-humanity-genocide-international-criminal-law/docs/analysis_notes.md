# International Criminal Law Analysis Notes

International criminal law differs from general state responsibility because it assigns legal responsibility to individuals. Analysis should move from crime category to contextual element, underlying act, mental element, and mode of liability. War crimes require an armed-conflict nexus. Crimes against humanity require a widespread or systematic attack against a civilian population. Genocide requires specific intent to destroy a protected group in whole or in part. Aggression is a leadership crime tied to the unlawful use of force by a state.

Common research errors include treating all atrocities as genocide, overlooking contextual elements, assuming command rank proves command responsibility, and ignoring admissibility or cooperation limits.
