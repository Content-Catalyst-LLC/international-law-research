# Public vs Private International Law Comparison

## Primary actors
- Public international law: States, international organizations, peoples, and some non-state actors
- Private international law: Individuals, corporations, families, commercial parties, and private litigants
- Overlap or tension: Investment law and arbitration often connect private claimants to public obligations.

## Core questions
- Public international law: What are the obligations of states and international legal subjects?
- Private international law: Which court has jurisdiction, which law applies, and will a judgment be recognized?
- Overlap or tension: Jurisdiction appears in both fields but performs different functions.

## Sources
- Public international law: Treaties, custom, general principles, judicial decisions, scholarly writings
- Private international law: Domestic conflict rules, private international law statutes, Hague conventions, party agreements
- Overlap or tension: Treaties can structure both public obligations and private litigation rules.

## Institutions
- Public international law: ICJ, UN organs, treaty bodies, international criminal courts, arbitral tribunals
- Private international law: Domestic courts, HCCH instruments, arbitral institutions, recognition/enforcement systems
- Overlap or tension: ICSID and investment arbitration are important hybrid institutions.

## Typical disputes
- Public international law: Use of force, treaties, human rights, state responsibility, law of the sea
- Private international law: Contracts, torts, family law, property, commercial disputes, foreign judgments
- Overlap or tension: Global supply chains and digital commerce increasingly blur the boundary.
