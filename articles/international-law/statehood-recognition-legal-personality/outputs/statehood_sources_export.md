## Statehood and Legal Personality Concepts

- **Statehood** (core doctrine) — Legal status of being a state for purposes of international law.
- **International legal personality** (core doctrine) — Capacity to possess international rights and duties and act on the international plane.
- **Permanent population** (Montevideo criterion) — Stable human community associated with the entity.
- **Defined territory** (Montevideo criterion) — Territorial core over which public authority is asserted.
- **Government** (Montevideo criterion) — Organized public authority capable of administering the entity.
- **Capacity to enter into relations** (Montevideo criterion) — External capacity and independence to conduct international relations.
- **Recognition** (recognition doctrine) — Acknowledgment by other states that an entity is to be treated as a state.
- **Declaratory theory** (recognition doctrine) — View that recognition acknowledges existing statehood rather than creating it.
- **Constitutive theory** (recognition doctrine) — View that recognition helps bring statehood into legal existence.
- **Non-recognition** (recognition doctrine) — Refusal to recognize unlawful or contested situations.
- **UN membership** (institutional standing) — Membership under Article 4 of the UN Charter.
- **Non-member observer State** (institutional standing) — UN observer-state status short of full membership.
- **Functional personality** (legal personality) — Legal capacity allocated according to institutional functions and purposes.
- **Self-determination** (peoplehood and status) — Right of peoples to determine political status and pursue development.
- **Secession** (contested status) — Withdrawal from an existing state to form a new entity.
- **Unequal recognition** (critical method) — Recognition practice shaped by power politics empire occupation and selective institutional admission.
