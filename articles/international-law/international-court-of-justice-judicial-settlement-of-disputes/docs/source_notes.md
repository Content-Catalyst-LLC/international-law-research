# Source Notes: The International Court of Justice and the Judicial Settlement of Disputes

## Primary source priorities

The article should cite the ICJ Statute, Rules of Court, UN Charter, official ICJ case pages, and official UN documents before relying on commentary. Commentary should be used to explain debates over consent, jurisdiction, advisory opinions, provisional measures, compliance, and the Court’s institutional role.

## Case-use notes

- **Nicaragua**: Use for self-defense, intervention, attribution, and customary international law.
- **Bosnia Genocide**: Use for genocide, state responsibility, prevention, and attribution.
- **Wall Advisory Opinion**: Use for occupation, self-determination, human-rights obligations, humanitarian law, and third-state obligations.
- **Kosovo Advisory Opinion**: Use for unilateral declarations of independence and the limits of advisory reasoning.
- **Chagos Advisory Opinion**: Use for decolonization and self-determination.
- **Occupied Palestinian Territory Advisory Opinion**: Use for legal consequences of occupation, non-recognition, and non-assistance.
- **South Africa v. Israel**: Use for contemporary provisional-measures practice under the Genocide Convention.

## Editorial caution

Avoid describing advisory opinions as if they were ordinary judgments between disputing states. Avoid overstating ICJ precedent as formally binding beyond the parties. Avoid treating the absence of enforcement machinery as proof that ICJ decisions are irrelevant. The Court’s authority is legal, institutional, diplomatic, and cumulative.
