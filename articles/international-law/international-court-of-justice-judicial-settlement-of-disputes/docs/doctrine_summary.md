# Doctrine Summary: The International Court of Justice and the Judicial Settlement of Disputes

The International Court of Justice is the principal judicial organ of the United Nations. It performs two main functions: deciding contentious disputes submitted by states and issuing advisory opinions requested by authorized UN organs and specialized agencies.

## Core points

1. **Charter foundation** — UN Charter Article 92 establishes the ICJ as the principal judicial organ of the United Nations. Article 94 addresses the duty of parties to comply with ICJ judgments.
2. **Contentious jurisdiction** — The Court hears disputes between states only where jurisdiction exists through consent, including special agreements, compromissory clauses, optional-clause declarations, or forum prorogatum.
3. **Advisory jurisdiction** — The Court may issue advisory opinions on legal questions requested by authorized UN organs and agencies under Article 65 of the ICJ Statute. Advisory opinions are not ordinary contentious judgments, but they can strongly influence legal interpretation, institutional practice, and state conduct.
4. **Provisional measures** — Article 41 allows the Court to indicate measures preserving rights pending a final decision where urgency, irreparable prejudice, and prima facie jurisdiction are present.
5. **Sources of law** — Article 38 frames the Court’s use of treaties, custom, general principles, judicial decisions, and scholarly writings.
6. **Judgments and precedent** — Article 59 states that ICJ decisions bind only the parties and only in respect of the particular case, but ICJ reasoning remains highly persuasive across international law.
7. **Limits** — The Court lacks a centralized enforcement arm and operates within a decentralized legal order shaped by consent, power, compliance politics, and institutional constraints.

## Analytical frame

A strong ICJ analysis should separate jurisdiction, admissibility, applicable law, merits, remedies, provisional measures, and compliance. The Court’s influence is strongest when its legal reasoning is taken up by states, UN organs, domestic courts, treaty bodies, scholars, civil society, and future litigants.
