## Adjudication Concepts

- **International adjudication** (system structure) — Judicial or quasi-judicial resolution and interpretation of international legal disputes.
- **Contentious jurisdiction** (ICJ doctrine) — Jurisdiction over disputes between states based on consent.
- **Advisory opinion** (ICJ doctrine) — Authoritative legal opinion requested by authorized UN organs or agencies.
- **Consent** (jurisdiction principle) — State acceptance of jurisdiction through treaty declaration or special agreement.
- **International criminal responsibility** (criminal accountability) — Individual responsibility for grave international crimes.
- **Complementarity** (ICC doctrine) — ICC operates as a court of last resort complementary to national jurisdictions.
- **Specialized tribunal** (institutional design) — Court with subject-matter competence such as ITLOS for law of the sea.
- **Regional human-rights court** (institutional design) — Court reviewing state compliance with regional human-rights instruments.
- **Ad hoc tribunal** (criminal accountability) — Tribunal established for particular conflicts or atrocity contexts.
- **Hybrid tribunal** (criminal accountability) — Court combining domestic and international elements.
- **WTO dispute settlement** (economic adjudication) — Trade dispute-settlement mechanism under WTO agreements.
- **Investor-state arbitration** (economic adjudication) — International forum for investor claims against states under investment treaties.
- **Enforcement limits** (implementation problem) — Weak or politically mediated implementation of judgments and awards.
- **Selective justice** (critical method) — Unequal access and enforcement shaped by geopolitical power.
- **International law from below** (critical method) — Use of international legality by marginalized peoples and anti-colonial movements.
- **Palestine** (contemporary adjudication) — Central case for advisory opinions recognition occupation and third-state obligations.
