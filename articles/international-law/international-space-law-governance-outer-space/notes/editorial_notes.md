# Editorial Notes

## Article role in the series

This article follows the International Health Law article and opens the outer-space component of the specialized and emerging-domain cluster. It should function as a deep doctrinal and governance map rather than a short overview.

## Key framing

- Space law is not simply a technical field for satellites and launch vehicles. It is a legal architecture for a shared domain beyond national territory.
- The Outer Space Treaty creates the constitutional baseline: non-appropriation, peaceful use, benefit of all countries, state responsibility, authorization and supervision, liability, and due regard.
- Private commercial activity does not remove state responsibility. Article VI remains central.
- Space debris, orbital congestion, spectrum allocation, lunar resources, military dual use, cyber vulnerability, and commercial concentration are governance pressure points.
- The article should connect space law to commons governance, security, sustainability, environmental law, commercial regulation, and global inequality.
