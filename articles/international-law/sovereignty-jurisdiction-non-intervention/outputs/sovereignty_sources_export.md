## Sovereignty and Jurisdiction Concepts

- **Sovereignty** (core doctrine) — Juridical independence and lawful authority of the state in international law.
- **Sovereign equality** (Charter principle) — Formal juridical equality of states under the Charter order.
- **Jurisdiction** (legal competence) — Authority to prescribe adjudicate and enforce law.
- **Prescriptive jurisdiction** (jurisdiction category) — Authority to make legal rules.
- **Adjudicative jurisdiction** (jurisdiction category) — Authority of courts and tribunals to decide cases.
- **Enforcement jurisdiction** (jurisdiction category) — Authority to compel compliance through coercive or administrative measures.
- **Territorial jurisdiction** (jurisdiction basis) — Authority over persons property acts and events within territory.
- **Nationality principle** (jurisdiction basis) — Authority over nationals abroad.
- **Passive personality principle** (jurisdiction basis) — Authority based on victim nationality in some contexts.
- **Protective principle** (jurisdiction basis) — Authority over conduct threatening security or core governmental interests.
- **Universal jurisdiction** (jurisdiction basis) — Limited jurisdiction over grave offenses regardless of territorial or nationality links.
- **Lotus principle** (jurisdiction theory) — Classical idea that restrictions on state independence cannot be presumed.
- **Non-intervention** (core doctrine) — Prohibition on coercive intervention in matters reserved to state freedom.
- **Coercion** (intervention element) — Defining element of prohibited intervention under Nicaragua.
- **Use of force** (Charter rule) — Threat or use of force against territorial integrity or political independence.
- **Domestic jurisdiction** (Charter concept) — Matters essentially within domestic jurisdiction subject to Charter exceptions.
- **Self-determination** (peoplehood and status) — Right of peoples to determine political status without external interference.
- **Selective enforcement** (critical method) — Unequal application of sovereignty non-intervention and force rules.
