## Customary-Law Concepts

- **Customary international law** (source framework) — Unwritten international law emerging from general practice accepted as law.
- **State practice** (custom element) — Conduct attributable to states including physical and verbal acts.
- **Opinio juris** (custom element) — Acceptance that a practice is carried out as a matter of legal right or obligation.
- **Generality** (practice assessment) — Extent to which practice is sufficiently widespread and representative.
- **Consistency** (practice assessment) — Extent to which practice is sufficiently coherent and not materially contradictory.
- **Representativeness** (practice assessment) — Whether relevant states and regions are meaningfully represented in the practice record.
- **International courts** (subsidiary means) — Courts and tribunals identifying custom through evidence and legal reasoning.
- **Persistent objector** (custom exception) — State that clearly and consistently objects to an emerging rule during formation.
- **Particular custom** (scope) — Regional or special customary rule among a defined group of states.
- **Specially affected states** (weighting problem) — States especially engaged in or affected by a practice may have relevant evidence but not exclusive authority.
- **Treaty and custom interaction** (source interaction) — Treaties may codify crystallize or help generate custom.
- **Soft law and custom** (normative development) — Resolutions and declarations may provide evidence or help shape opinio juris.
- **Colonial critique** (critical method) — Customary law formation must account for exclusion empire and unequal participation.
