# Quote Log — Customary International Law

Use this file to track short primary-source excerpts used in the article.

## Current quote candidates

### ICJ Statute, Article 38(1)(b)

Excerpt: “evidence of a general practice accepted as law”

Editorial purpose: Defines customary international law in the classical source framework.

### ILC Draft Conclusions, Conclusion 2

Excerpt: “a general practice that is accepted as law (opinio juris)”

Editorial purpose: Clarifies the two-element method for identifying custom.

### ILC Draft Conclusions, Conclusion 4

Excerpt: “forms of State practice include diplomatic acts and correspondence”

Editorial purpose: Shows that state practice includes both physical and verbal conduct.

### ILC Draft Conclusions, Conclusion 9

Excerpt: “undertaken with a sense of legal right or obligation”

Editorial purpose: Clarifies opinio juris as the legal-belief element.

### ILC Draft Conclusions, Conclusion 10

Excerpt: “may take a wide range of forms”

Editorial purpose: Identifies the range of evidence that may support opinio juris.

### ICJ, North Sea Continental Shelf

Excerpt: “a settled practice” and “carried out in such a way, as to be evidence of a belief that this practice is rendered obligatory”

Editorial purpose: Classic judicial formulation of practice and legal obligation.

### ICJ, Nicaragua

Excerpt: “customary international law continues to exist and to apply, separately from international treaty law”

Editorial purpose: Shows that customary rules may operate independently alongside treaty rules.

### ICJ, Jurisdictional Immunities

Excerpt: “State immunity derives from the principle of sovereign equality of States”

Editorial purpose: Connects customary-law analysis to state immunity and sovereign equality.
