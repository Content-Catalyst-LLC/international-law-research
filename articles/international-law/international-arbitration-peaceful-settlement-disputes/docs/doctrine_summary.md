# Doctrine Summary — International Arbitration and the Peaceful Settlement of Disputes

International arbitration is a consent-based method of dispute settlement. It differs from permanent judicial settlement because parties generally participate in selecting the forum, procedure, arbitrators, applicable rules, and sometimes confidentiality or transparency arrangements.

Core doctrinal issues include consent, jurisdiction, applicable law, arbitrator appointment, independence and impartiality, admissibility, evidence, provisional measures, award finality, annulment or set-aside, recognition, enforcement, and public-interest legitimacy.

In public international law, arbitration is one of the peaceful means listed in Article 33 of the UN Charter. It appears in state-to-state disputes, boundary disputes, maritime delimitation, UNCLOS Annex VII proceedings, mixed claims, and treaty-based dispute settlement. In private and mixed regimes, arbitration also appears through commercial arbitration, investor-state arbitration, ICSID, UNCITRAL, and the New York Convention framework.
