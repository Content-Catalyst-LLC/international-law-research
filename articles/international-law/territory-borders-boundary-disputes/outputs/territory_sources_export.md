## Core Concepts

- **Territory** (foundational concept) — Spatial domain over which a state exercises legal authority.
- **Territorial sovereignty** (foundational doctrine) — Exclusive state authority over territory subject to international law.
- **Territorial integrity** (Charter principle) — Protection of state territory against unlawful force or annexation.
- **Boundary** (legal line) — Line separating the territorial authority of neighboring states.
- **Frontier** (border zone) — Broader borderland or transition zone sometimes distinguished from a fixed legal boundary.
- **Title to territory** (territorial claim) — Legal basis for sovereignty over territory.
- **Effectivités** (evidence) — Effective exercise of state authority used as evidence of sovereignty.
- **Critical date** (evidentiary doctrine) — Date after which acts may carry reduced evidentiary weight in a dispute.
- **Intertemporal law** (temporal doctrine) — Evaluation of territorial title according to law at relevant historical times.
- **Uti possidetis juris** (decolonization doctrine) — Transformation of colonial administrative boundaries into international boundaries at independence.
- **Delimitation** (boundary process) — Legal description or determination of a boundary line.
- **Demarcation** (boundary process) — Physical marking of the boundary on the ground.
- **Maps** (evidence) — Cartographic materials used as evidence but not always title in themselves.
- **Natural boundaries** (boundary type) — Rivers watersheds mountains and other physical features used in boundary definition.
- **Maritime delimitation** (law of the sea) — Determination of maritime boundaries between overlapping claims.
- **Island sovereignty** (territorial issue) — Determination of sovereignty over islands rocks reefs or maritime features.
- **Occupation** (belligerent situation) — Temporary authority over territory by hostile armed forces without transfer of sovereignty.
- **Annexation** (unlawful acquisition) — Claimed incorporation of territory acquired by force or coercion.
- **Self-determination** (peoplehood right) — Right of peoples to determine political status and development.
- **Borderlands** (critical lens) — Communities and ecologies shaped by borders often ignored by state-centered maps.
