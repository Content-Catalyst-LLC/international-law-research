## General-Principles Concepts

- **Article 38(1)(c)** (source framework) — Classical source provision recognizing general principles of law.
- **Recognition by the community of nations** (method) — Modern recognition requirement in ILC draft conclusions.
- **Principles derived from national legal systems** (method) — General principles identified through comparative analysis across legal systems.
- **Principles formed within the international legal system** (method) — General principles arising from international law's own institutional and juridical structure.
- **Good faith** (substantive principle) — Principle supporting treaty performance interpretation and legal integrity.
- **Estoppel** (substantive principle) — Principle preventing opportunistic inconsistency where reliance is legally relevant.
- **Abuse of rights** (substantive principle) — Principle limiting formal legal powers exercised contrary to the purpose of the legal order.
- **Res judicata** (procedural principle) — Finality of judgments and protection against relitigation.
- **Procedural fairness** (procedural principle) — Fair hearing equality and adjudicative legitimacy.
- **Reparation** (remedial principle) — Legal consequence of injury or wrongful act.
- **Non liquet** (gap-filling problem) — Problem of judicial inability to decide because law appears incomplete.
- **Comparative method** (method) — Disciplined comparison across legal systems to identify genuinely general principles.
- **Colonial hierarchy** (critical method) — Historical critique of whose legal traditions counted as general or civilized.
