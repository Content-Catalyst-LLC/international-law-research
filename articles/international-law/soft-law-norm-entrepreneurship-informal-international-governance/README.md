# Soft Law, Norm Entrepreneurship, and Informal International Governance

This folder supports the Sustainable Catalyst International Law article:

**Soft Law, Norm Entrepreneurship, and Informal International Governance**

The folder is intentionally limited to research and support materials. It does **not** store the full WordPress HTML article. WordPress remains the publication layer; GitHub remains the research, metadata, source, and structured-output layer.

## Contents

- `metadata/article_metadata.json` — title, slug, sequence, SEO metadata, and repository status.
- `sources/primary_authorities.md` — primary instruments and institutional materials.
- `sources/further_reading.md` — scholarly and institutional reading list.
- `data/soft_law_instruments_matrix.csv` — soft-law instrument categories and legal functions.
- `data/norm_entrepreneurship_workflow.csv` — norm-development pathway from proposal to legal influence.
- `data/informal_governance_risk_matrix.csv` — legitimacy, capture, accountability, and implementation risks.
- `sql/soft_law_schema.sql` — simple schema for cataloguing instruments, actors, and governance effects.
- `python/build_soft_law_outputs.py` — builds structured JSON support outputs from the CSV files.
- `outputs/` — generated JSON summaries and indexes.

## Editorial note

This article examines soft law as a central technique of contemporary international governance: declarations, guidelines, principles, technical standards, model laws, institutional recommendations, voluntary compacts, expert frameworks, and informal coordination mechanisms. It treats soft law as neither legally meaningless nor automatically legitimate. Instead, it analyzes how non-binding instruments shape expectations, interpretation, compliance, institutional behavior, market conduct, and eventual hard-law development.
