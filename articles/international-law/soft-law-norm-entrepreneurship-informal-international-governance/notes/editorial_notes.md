# Editorial Notes

This article should be framed as a bridge between formal international law and the informal governance practices that increasingly shape global behavior. It should avoid two oversimplifications:

1. Soft law is not “fake law” or legally irrelevant.
2. Soft law is not automatically legitimate simply because it is flexible, expert-driven, or consensual.

The article should emphasize how soft law works through interpretation, expectation, institutional practice, domestic implementation, procurement, certification, peer review, reporting, reputational pressure, treaty development, and market coordination. It should also emphasize the risks: capture, democratic deficit, inequality of participation, opacity, legitimacy laundering, and avoidance of binding accountability.
