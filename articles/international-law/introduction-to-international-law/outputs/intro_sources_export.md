## Core Concepts

- **International law** (field) — Legal framework governing relations among states international organizations peoples individuals and other global actors.
- **Public international law** (field) — Law governing states international organizations and other international legal subjects.
- **Private international law** (field) — Conflict-of-laws rules for cross-border private disputes.
- **Domestic law** (field) — Law operating within a particular state.
- **Sovereign equality** (Charter principle) — Formal juridical equality of UN members under Article 2(1).
- **Horizontal legal order** (system structure) — Decentralized legal order without a single world sovereign.
- **Treaties** (source) — Written international agreements governed by international law.
- **Customary international law** (source) — General practice accepted as law.
- **General principles** (source) — Principles recognized across legal systems and used in international adjudication.
- **Judicial decisions** (subsidiary means) — Decisions used to identify and clarify rules.
- **Scholarly writings** (subsidiary means) — Teachings of highly qualified publicists as subsidiary means.
- **International organizations** (institutional actor) — Organizations such as the United Nations created by states and endowed with legal functions.
- **International legal personality** (legal concept) — Capacity to bear rights obligations or functions under international law.
- **Compliance** (system function) — How international law influences behavior through institutions reciprocity reputation and enforcement.
- **Selective enforcement** (critical lens) — Unequal application of rules depending on power and institutional politics.
- **Everyday international law** (practical lens) — International law's role in aviation shipping communications public health trade and mobility.
