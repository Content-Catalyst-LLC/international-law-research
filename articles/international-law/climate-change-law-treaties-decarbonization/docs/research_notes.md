# Research notes — Climate Change Law, Treaties, and the Legal Politics of Decarbonization

This article treats climate change law as a layered governance regime rather than a single treaty obligation. The framework connects the UNFCCC, Kyoto Protocol, Paris Agreement, COP/CMA decisions, NDCs, transparency, finance, loss and damage, human rights, advisory opinions, and domestic implementation.

Key editorial emphasis:

1. Climate law is not just emissions accounting; it is a legal architecture for planetary transformation.
2. Paris combines legally binding procedural duties with nationally determined substantive ambition.
3. Decarbonization is legally contested because it affects energy systems, labor, development, trade, investment, land, finance, and rights.
4. Climate justice requires attention to historical emissions, capacity, vulnerability, adaptation, loss and damage, and unequal transition costs.
5. Recent advisory opinions strengthen the connection between climate harm, due diligence, marine environmental duties, and human rights.
