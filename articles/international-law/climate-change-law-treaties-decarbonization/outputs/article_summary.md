# Climate Change Law, Treaties, and the Legal Politics of Decarbonization

**Focus keyword:** climate change law
**Slug:** `climate-change-law-treaties-decarbonization`

## Excerpt

Climate change law, treaties, and the legal politics of decarbonization explain how international law organizes the global response to greenhouse-gas emissions, fossil-fuel dependence, adaptation, and loss and damage. This article examines the UNFCCC, Kyoto Protocol, Paris Agreement, nationally determined contributions, global stocktake, mitigation, adaptation, climate finance, technology transfer, transparency, carbon markets, just transition, human rights, advisory opinions, and the contested politics of decarbonization. It shows how climate law combines binding obligations, procedural duties, soft-law pressure, domestic implementation, scientific assessment, and diplomatic bargaining. The article also addresses fossil-fuel phaseout, climate litigation, trade measures, investment disputes, unequal responsibility, vulnerable states, and climate justice. By connecting doctrine, institutions, case studies, and lawyer-facing workflows, it shows how climate law governs planetary transformation while exposing the limits of consensus-based treaty politics under contemporary international law.
