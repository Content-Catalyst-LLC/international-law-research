## State Responsibility Concepts

- **Internationally wrongful act** (core doctrine) — Conduct attributable to a state that breaches an international obligation.
- **Attribution** (core doctrine) — Legal connection between conduct and the state.
- **Breach** (core doctrine) — Conduct not in conformity with an international obligation binding on the state.
- **State organ** (attribution) — Organ whose conduct is treated as an act of the state under international law.
- **Direction or control** (attribution) — State responsibility for conduct of actors acting on instructions or under direction or control.
- **Aid or assistance** (complicity) — Responsibility for knowingly aiding or assisting another state in wrongful conduct.
- **Omission** (responsibility by failure) — Failure to act where international law requires prevention warning control investigation or remedy.
- **Due diligence** (standard of conduct) — Required vigilance prevention control and response in specific legal contexts.
- **Circumstances precluding wrongfulness** (excuse doctrine) — Limited grounds that preclude wrongfulness without extinguishing the obligation.
- **Necessity** (exceptional doctrine) — Strictly limited doctrine involving grave and imminent peril and no lawful alternative.
- **Countermeasures** (implementation) — Temporary conditioned measures intended to induce compliance by responsible state.
- **Full reparation** (consequence) — Obligation to repair injury caused by internationally wrongful act.
- **Serious breach** (community interest) — Breach of an obligation arising under a peremptory norm involving gross or systematic failure.
- **Non-recognition** (third-state duty) — Duty not to recognize as lawful a situation created by serious breach.
- **Non-assistance** (third-state duty) — Duty not to aid or assist in maintaining a situation created by serious breach.
- **Selective enforcement** (critical method) — Unequal invocation and implementation of responsibility depending on power politics.
