# The High Seas, Deep Seabed Governance, and the Global Commons

Slug: `high-seas-deep-seabed-global-commons`

Focus keyword: **high seas**

The high seas, deep seabed governance, and the global commons explain how international law governs ocean spaces beyond national jurisdiction. This article examines UNCLOS high-seas freedoms, flag-state responsibility, due regard, submarine cables, fisheries, marine biodiversity, the deep seabed Area, common heritage of humankind, the International Seabed Authority, sponsoring-state obligations, environmental impact assessment, precaution, marine genetic resources, BBNJ governance, benefit sharing, capacity building, and technology transfer. It analyzes how global commons law balances freedom, extraction, stewardship, equity, science, and institutional authority while addressing climate change, deep seabed mining, biodiversity loss, illegal fishing, weak flag-state control, and unequal technological capacity. By connecting doctrine, institutions, case studies, and lawyer-facing workflows, it shows how ocean commons governance tests whether international law can protect shared planetary systems before ecological harm becomes irreversible.
