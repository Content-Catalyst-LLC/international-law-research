# Doctrine Summary

International humanitarian law regulates the conduct of parties to armed conflict. It applies independently of the legality of the initial resort to force. Its major sources include the four Geneva Conventions of 1949, the Additional Protocols of 1977, the Hague tradition, customary IHL, international criminal law, and jurisprudence of international and domestic tribunals.

Key analytical steps include conflict classification, identification of applicable treaty and customary rules, protected-person status, targeting analysis, occupation analysis, humanitarian relief obligations, detention safeguards, and accountability pathways.
