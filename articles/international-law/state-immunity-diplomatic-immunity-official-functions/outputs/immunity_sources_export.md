## Immunity Concepts

- **State immunity** (core doctrine) — Immunity of a state and its property from foreign domestic jurisdiction.
- **Immunity from execution** (state immunity) — Protection against enforcement measures against state property.
- **Restrictive immunity** (state immunity) — Doctrine distinguishing sovereign acts from commercial or private-law acts.
- **Acta jure imperii** (state immunity) — Sovereign or governmental acts for which immunity is strongest.
- **Acta jure gestionis** (state immunity) — Commercial or private-law acts for which immunity is often restricted.
- **Waiver** (exception) — State consent to foreign jurisdiction or execution in defined circumstances.
- **Diplomatic immunity** (diplomatic law) — Privileges and immunities protecting diplomatic missions and agents.
- **Diplomatic inviolability** (diplomatic law) — Special protection of mission premises diplomatic agents and archives.
- **Consular immunity** (consular law) — Narrower and more functional immunity for consular officers and consular acts.
- **Internationally protected persons** (protected persons) — Protected category including certain officials and diplomatic agents under the 1973 Convention.
- **Official immunity** (official functions) — Immunity of state officials from foreign jurisdiction in certain circumstances.
- **Immunity ratione personae** (official immunity) — Personal immunity of certain serving high-ranking officials.
- **Immunity ratione materiae** (official immunity) — Functional immunity for official acts that may persist after office.
- **Arrest Warrant** (ICJ case) — Leading case on serving foreign minister immunity.
- **Jurisdictional Immunities** (ICJ case) — Leading case on state immunity despite grave allegations in foreign civil proceedings.
- **Tehran Hostages** (ICJ case) — Leading case on diplomatic and consular inviolability.
- **Damascus consular strike** (contemporary case) — Attack on Iranian consular building in Damascus as test of official protection and diplomatic premises.
- **Grave international crimes** (accountability) — Genocide crimes against humanity war crimes torture apartheid aggression and related accountability tensions.
- **Palestine** (contemporary accountability) — Example of official protection procedural barriers and geopolitical shielding in relation to occupation and atrocity claims.
- **Selective enforcement** (critical method) — Unequal application of immunity accountability and diplomatic protection rules.
