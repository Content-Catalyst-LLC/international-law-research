## Critical framing

The article should avoid treating immunity as mere privilege. It should explain immunity as a procedural and functional protection tied to sovereign equality, diplomatic communication, official representation, and interstate order. It should also foreground the danger that immunity becomes a shield for impunity when official conduct is connected to atrocity, occupation, aggression, torture, apartheid, or domination. The Damascus strike should be treated as a central contemporary test of diplomatic and consular protection, not as a marginal example.
