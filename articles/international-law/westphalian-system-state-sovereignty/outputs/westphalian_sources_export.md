## Sovereignty Concepts

- **Westphalian system** (historical-symbolic framework) — Shorthand for a state-centered order associated with territorial sovereignty and formal independence.
- **Peace of Westphalia** (historical settlement) — 1648 peace settlement including Osnabruck and Munster treaties.
- **Thirty Years War** (historical context) — Destructive early modern European conflict ending with Westphalian settlements.
- **Territorial sovereignty** (sovereignty doctrine) — Primary authority exercised within defined territorial boundaries.
- **Sovereign equality** (Charter principle) — Juridical equality of states regardless of material power.
- **Non-intervention** (Charter principle) — Prohibition on intervention in internal or external affairs of another state.
- **Domestic jurisdiction** (Charter principle) — Matters essentially within a state's domestic jurisdiction subject to Charter limits.
- **Statehood** (statehood doctrine) — Legal personality of a state associated with population territory government and capacity.
- **Recognition** (statehood doctrine) — Legal and political act acknowledging statehood or government status.
- **Westphalian myth** (critical historiography) — Caution against treating 1648 as a fully formed origin of modern sovereignty.
- **Empire and globalization** (critical method) — Expansion of the European state system through colonization unequal treaties and global legal hierarchy.
- **Decolonization** (legal transformation) — Universalization and transformation of sovereign equality through anti-colonial struggle and UN membership.
- **Hybrid order** (contemporary structure) — State-centered legal order qualified by institutions rights global governance and interdependence.
