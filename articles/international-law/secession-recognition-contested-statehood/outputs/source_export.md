## Concepts
- **Statehood** — Legal status of an entity as a state in international law
- **Secession** — Withdrawal of a territory or people from an existing state
- **Recognition** — Acceptance by other states of an entity's legal personality or government
- **Montevideo criteria** — Permanent population defined territory government and capacity for relations
- **Declaratory theory** — Statehood exists if objective criteria are met regardless of recognition
- **Constitutive theory** — Statehood depends significantly on recognition by existing states
- **Contested statehood** — Entities with some state-like features but disputed or limited recognition
- **Non-recognition** — Duty or practice of refusing recognition of unlawful territorial situations
- **Territorial integrity** — Protection of existing states against force and unilateral dismemberment
- **Self-determination** — Right of peoples to determine political status and development
- **Remedial secession** — Claim that extreme oppression may justify external self-determination
- **UN membership** — Admission requiring Security Council recommendation and General Assembly decision

## Contested Statehood Examples
- **Kosovo** — Declaration of independence and partial recognition (ICJ advisory opinion narrow holding; UNSC 1244 context)
- **Palestine** — Widespread recognition and UN non-member observer State status (Self-determination occupation recognition and Security Council politics)
- **Northern Cyprus** — Declared entity recognized only by Turkey (UNSC 541 and 550 declared secessionist actions legally invalid)
- **Somaliland** — Effective internal governance with limited recognition (Effectiveness recognition African Union and Somalia territorial integrity)
- **Taiwan** — Self-governing democracy with limited formal diplomatic recognition (UNGA 2758 representation of China and contested status)
- **South Sudan** — Secession by referendum and broad recognition (Peace agreement referendum independence and UN membership)
- **Chagos/Mauritius** — Decolonization and territorial detachment (ICJ advisory opinion on unlawful separation)
- **East Timor** — Occupation and eventual independence (ICJ erga omnes language and later referendum/independence)
