# Research Notes: Nuremberg, Tokyo, and the Historical Development of International Criminal Justice

This article traces the historical development of international criminal justice from postwar tribunals through ad hoc, hybrid, and permanent institutions. The research emphasis is on doctrine, institution-building, legality, selectivity, colonial silences, and the transition from exceptional tribunals to a broader accountability architecture.

Key concepts:

- Individual criminal responsibility
- Crimes against peace / aggression
- War crimes
- Crimes against humanity
- Genocide
- Official position and superior orders
- Command responsibility
- Victor's justice and legality debates
- Tribunal legacy and institutional memory
