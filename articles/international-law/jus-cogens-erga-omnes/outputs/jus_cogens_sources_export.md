## Jus Cogens and Erga Omnes Concepts

- **Jus cogens** (normative hierarchy) — Peremptory norm of general international law from which no derogation is permitted.
- **Erga omnes** (legal interest) — Obligations owed to the international community as a whole.
- **Erga omnes partes** (treaty-community interest) — Obligations owed to all parties of a multilateral treaty.
- **Non-derogability** (hierarchy) — Inability of states to contract out of or derogate from a peremptory norm.
- **Treaty invalidity** (legal consequence) — Treaties conflicting with jus cogens are void.
- **Emergence of new peremptory norms** (legal consequence) — Existing treaties conflicting with a new peremptory norm become void and terminate.
- **Non-recognition** (third-state duty) — Duty not to recognize as lawful a situation created by serious breach.
- **Non-assistance** (third-state duty) — Duty not to aid or assist in maintaining a situation created by serious breach.
- **Serious breach** (state responsibility) — Gross or systematic failure to fulfill an obligation arising under a peremptory norm.
- **Self-determination** (peremptory norm/right) — Right of peoples to determine their political status and pursue development.
- **Apartheid** (peremptory norm example) — Institutionalized racial domination and systematic oppression.
- **Aggression** (peremptory norm example) — Unlawful aggressive use of force.
- **Genocide** (peremptory norm example) — Destruction of protected groups under international law.
- **Torture** (peremptory norm example) — Prohibited severe pain or suffering inflicted for prohibited purposes.
