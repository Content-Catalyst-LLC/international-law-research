# Source Notes — Sources of International Law

## Core primary authorities

1. Statute of the International Court of Justice, especially Article 38
2. Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties
3. International Law Commission materials on customary international law
4. International Law Commission materials on general principles of law
5. UN Charter, especially provisions shaping post-1945 legal order
6. ICJ decisions and advisory opinions as subsidiary means and interpretive authorities
7. UN General Assembly resolutions, institutional practice, and soft-law materials where relevant
8. ILC materials on jus cogens, fragmentation, state responsibility, and legal hierarchy

## Critical framing

Sources doctrine should not be presented as politically neutral. The article should explain the formal source categories clearly while also examining who historically counted as a legal subject, whose state practice counted as general practice, whose legal traditions were treated as recognized principles, and whose scholarship was treated as authoritative. Source doctrine should be framed as both legal method and contested legal history.
