## Treaty-Doctrine Concepts

- **Reservation** (reservations) — Unilateral statement purporting to exclude or modify legal effect of treaty provisions.
- **Object and purpose test** (reservations) — Substantive permissibility limit on reservations under VCLT Article 19(c).
- **Acceptance of reservation** (reservations) — Consent by another party to treaty relations as modified by the reservation.
- **Objection to reservation** (reservations) — Response by a treaty party opposing the reservation and potentially affecting treaty relations.
- **Interpretative declaration** (reservations) — Statement clarifying a state's understanding of treaty meaning without purporting to modify legal effect.
- **Good faith** (interpretation) — Normative condition governing treaty interpretation and performance.
- **Ordinary meaning** (interpretation) — Textual meaning of treaty terms read in context.
- **Object and purpose** (interpretation) — Treaty's central aim used to orient interpretation.
- **Supplementary means** (interpretation) — Preparatory work and circumstances of conclusion used under Article 32.
- **Multiple authentic texts** (interpretation) — Equal authority of multilingual treaty texts under Article 33.
- **Subsequent agreements** (interpretation) — Later agreements among parties regarding interpretation.
- **Subsequent practice** (interpretation) — Later practice establishing agreement of parties regarding interpretation.
- **Validity** (validity) — Legal effectiveness of treaty consent and treaty obligation.
- **Coercion of a state** (invalidity) — Treaty consent procured by threat or use of force.
- **Jus cogens invalidity** (invalidity) — Treaty void if conflicting with peremptory norm of general international law.
- **Unequal treaties** (critical method) — Treaty arrangements shaped by structural power asymmetry and compromised consent.
