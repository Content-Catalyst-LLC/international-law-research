## Fragmentation and Coherence Concepts

- **Fragmentation** (system structure) — Diversification of norms institutions and regimes that creates overlap tension or conflict.
- **Coherence** (system structure) — Intelligible legal ordering across differentiated regimes.
- **Regime complex** (institutional structure) — Partially overlapping set of norms institutions and procedures governing related subject matter.
- **Institutional pluralism** (institutional structure) — Multiplicity of courts tribunals treaty bodies institutions and domestic courts participating in international legal reasoning.
- **Norm conflict** (conflict problem) — Situation in which multiple valid norms appear to pull in different directions.
- **Systemic integration** (interpretive technique) — Reading treaty obligations in relation to relevant rules of international law applicable between the parties.
- **VCLT Article 31(3)(c)** (interpretive technique) — Treaty interpretation rule requiring relevant rules of international law to be taken into account.
- **Lex specialis** (conflict technique) — Use of the more specific rule where appropriate.
- **Lex posterior** (conflict technique) — Use of the later rule where appropriate.
- **Self-contained regime** (regime theory) — Dense specialized regime with its own rules remedies or institutions but not wholly insulated from general law.
- **Judicial multiplicity** (institutional structure) — Multiple adjudicative bodies developing and applying international law without a single appellate hierarchy.
- **Power asymmetry** (critical method) — Unequal institutional strength enforcement capacity expertise access and geopolitical power shaping coherence.
- **Coherence without uniformity** (normative method) — Maintaining systemic intelligibility while preserving legitimate specialization.
