# Research Notes

This article treats economic, social, and cultural rights as legal rights rather than mere policy aspirations. The key analytical distinctions are progressive realization, immediate obligations, maximum available resources, minimum core, non-discrimination, non-retrogression, and effective remedies.

Lawyer-facing analysis should connect legal doctrine to evidence: budgets, legislation, administrative practice, resource allocation, discriminatory impact, service access, domestic remedies, treaty-body materials, and institutional accountability.
