# Doctrine Summary: Occupation, Detention, and the Legal Governance of War-Torn Territory

This article explains how international humanitarian law governs territory and persons during armed conflict. The central legal distinction is between combat governance and sovereign title: occupation may give a hostile army temporary factual authority, but it does not confer sovereignty. The occupying power is constrained by Hague occupation law, Geneva Convention IV, customary IHL, human-rights law where applicable, and war-crimes rules.

Core legal questions include:

- whether occupation exists through effective control;
- which persons are protected;
- whether detention is authorized and reviewed;
- whether civilians have been unlawfully transferred, deported, or confined;
- whether humanitarian relief and ICRC access are respected;
- whether occupation has become annexationist, extractive, or permanent in character.
