# Doctrine Summary

Compliance and enforcement are not single doctrines. They are a set of pathways through which international legal decisions become practically meaningful: voluntary compliance, institutional supervision, domestic implementation, Security Council follow-up, third-state non-recognition duties, criminal cooperation, award enforcement, authorized retaliation, and public legal mobilization.

The central analytical distinction is between legal authority and coercive enforcement. International courts may clarify law with high authority even when coercive implementation remains politically constrained.
