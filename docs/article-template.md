# International Law Article Template

Use this structure for major International Law articles.

## Title

Use a precise, doctrine-aware title.

## Opening Thesis

Begin with a strong thesis paragraph explaining why the doctrine, regime, institution, or controversy matters to the international legal order.

## Series Context

Include a short note linking the article to the broader International Law knowledge series.

## Table of Contents

Use a clean HTML table of contents.

## Core Sections

Recommended section structure:

1. Legal Problem and Historical Background
2. Source Framework
3. Treaty Law and Institutional Architecture
4. Customary International Law and State Practice
5. Courts, Tribunals, and Interpretive Authority
6. Enforcement, Compliance, and Selective Application
7. Power, Inequality, and Critical Perspectives
8. Contemporary Developments
9. Conclusion
10. Further Reading
11. References

## Source Discipline

Use authoritative primary legal materials wherever possible. Use scholarship to interpret doctrine, historical development, and contested perspectives.

## Critical Orientation

Where relevant, foreground:

- Colonial and imperial legacies
- Unequal application of international law
- Selective enforcement
- Differentiated state capacity
- Global North and Global South perspectives
- Gendered, racialized, Indigenous, and postcolonial critiques
- The gap between formal universality and material power
