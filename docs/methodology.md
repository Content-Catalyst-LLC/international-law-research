# Methodology

This repository treats international law as doctrine, institutional practice, historical formation, and contested global order.

## Core Method

The research workflow uses structured metadata rather than republishing legal materials in full. Each record should identify the legal regime, source type, official source URL where possible, institutional context, date, and relevance to the article roadmap.

## Research Priorities

1. Use official primary sources wherever possible.
2. Distinguish binding law, soft law, institutional guidance, judicial interpretation, scholarly commentary, and critical theory.
3. Preserve historical context, including colonial and imperial legacies.
4. Track differentiated global perspectives, including Global South, postcolonial, feminist, Indigenous, regional, and critical legal approaches.
5. Avoid presenting international law as either perfectly neutral or purely rhetorical. Treat it as a real legal structure shaped by unequal power and uneven enforcement.

## Article Development

Each article should connect legal doctrine to:

- Source authority
- Institutional practice
- Enforcement conditions
- Historical formation
- Power asymmetries
- Selective application
- Differentiated state capacity
- Human consequences
- Global governance implications
