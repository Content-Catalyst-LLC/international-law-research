# Source Hierarchy for International Law Research

This hierarchy supports citation discipline and research quality.

## Tier 1: Primary Legal Materials

- Treaties and conventions
- UN Charter provisions
- Court and tribunal statutes
- Judgments
- Advisory opinions
- Orders and procedural decisions
- Customary international law studies from authoritative bodies
- Official treaty depositary records
- Official institutional documents

Examples of authoritative sources:

- United Nations Treaty Collection
- International Court of Justice
- International Criminal Court
- International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea
- International Committee of the Red Cross
- Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights
- United Nations Audiovisual Library of International Law
- Regional human rights courts and commissions

## Tier 2: Institutional Interpretation and Practice

- International Law Commission materials
- UN General Assembly and Security Council records
- Treaty body general comments and concluding observations
- Special rapporteur reports
- Institutional manuals and official commentaries
- State practice digests
- Regional organization records

## Tier 3: Scholarly Sources

- Leading treatises
- Peer-reviewed articles
- University press monographs
- Edited academic volumes
- Specialist legal commentaries

## Tier 4: Critical and Historical Sources

- Third World Approaches to International Law
- Postcolonial legal scholarship
- Feminist international law
- Indigenous legal perspectives
- Histories of empire, sovereignty, and international legal hierarchy
- Political economy of international law

## Source Use Principle

Primary sources establish legal authority. Secondary sources interpret, critique, contextualize, and compare. Critical sources are essential where doctrine intersects with power, colonial history, structural inequality, and selective enforcement.
